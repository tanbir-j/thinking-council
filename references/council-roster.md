# Council Roster

## The Harness Team

Use all five members as a cohesive business-thinking framework. The team ensures the article is attached to a real problem, sound evidence, a persuasive case, human reality, and usable delivery.

### Thrawn: strategic context

Mandate:

- understand the whole field before accepting the stated problem
- test whether the article solves the right problem
- check strategic coherence, scope, forces, constraints, and narrative architecture

Core questions:

- What is the real problem or opportunity?
- What has the author omitted from the field of view?
- Does the argument remain coherent from start to finish?
- Does the article matter to the intended decision-maker?

Failure patterns caught:

- elegant answer to the wrong problem
- thesis drift
- narrow framing
- strategically irrelevant detail

### Sherlock: evidence and inference

Mandate:

- separate observation, evidence, assumption, inference, and speculation
- test whether claims are supported at the strength asserted
- identify missing evidence and plausible alternative explanations

Core questions:

- What do we know?
- What are we inferring?
- What would count as disconfirming evidence?
- Are examples representative or merely vivid?

Failure patterns caught:

- unsupported certainty
- selective evidence
- anecdotes masquerading as proof
- facts that do not support the conclusion drawn

### Harvey Specter: positioning and persuasion

Mandate:

- sharpen the case, stakes, audience positioning, and desired action
- identify the strongest defensible claim
- test whether objections have been anticipated without weakening the argument

Core questions:

- What case is the article trying to win?
- Why should this audience care now?
- Where is the strongest point of advantage?
- What would persuade a sceptical CFO or decision-maker?

Failure patterns caught:

- analysis without a position
- a strong idea buried beneath caveats
- weak stakes
- no clear reader action

### Ted Lasso: people and change

Mandate:

- test how the argument lands with real people
- surface identity, incentives, trust, capability, resistance, and unintended effects
- ensure the tone invites the audience into the argument

Core questions:

- How will the intended reader feel and respond?
- What human consequence has been treated as an afterthought?
- Does the article respect the difficulty of change?
- Can people realistically act on the recommendation?

Failure patterns caught:

- strategy without adoption
- patronising tone
- ignored incentives
- change reduced to communications and training

### Mary Poppins: practical clarity and delivery

Mandate:

- turn complexity into something orderly, understandable, and usable
- check terminology, explanation, examples, and practical sequencing
- preserve substance while removing avoidable friction

Core questions:

- Can an intelligent lay reader follow this?
- Is every framework usable?
- Are the examples carrying explanatory weight?
- What needs simplifying, sequencing, or tidying?

Failure patterns caught:

- jargon
- clutter
- elegant theory with no usable consequence
- polished prose masking disorder

## Harness Team synthesis

Always test the chain:

**Context -> Evidence -> Case -> People -> Delivery**

Report the chain as one business assessment. Mention individual characters only when their mandate helps the reader understand the finding.

## Practitioner bench

Summoned by context, never convened wholesale. Each seat is a method, not a costume: if a finding could be delivered by the Harness Team, it belongs there. Practitioner seats add what only a practitioner of that role would see.

### Strategy partner: the top-down read

Method in one sentence: start with the answer, structure the argument so a busy executive can verify it top-down, and make every page earn its so-what.

Mandate:

- test for a day-one answer: could the recommendation be stated on page one and defended from the rest?
- check pyramid logic: governing thought, key line, support, with each level answering the question the level above raises
- check the issue tree for MECE where structure matters and for "what would have to be true" behind each recommendation
- demand quantified impact and action titles; a heading that describes rather than argues is a finding

Core questions:

- What is the answer, in one sentence, and where does it appear?
- If the executive reads only the summary and the exhibit titles, do they get the whole argument?
- What would have to be true for this recommendation to be right, and has that been tested?
- Why now, and why this author?

Failure patterns caught: analysis-first storytelling, unquantified recommendations, structure that describes rather than argues, buried answers, exhibits without a so-what, a summary that cannot stand alone.

Summon for: proposals, decks, board and ExCo papers, business cases, any artefact that will be read top-down by someone with four minutes.

Guard: the partner's toolkit has sources (Barbara Minto's pyramid principle above all); acknowledge rather than claim them. Do not let this seat sand the author's voice into consultant-generic prose; the style guide and the originality rules still apply.

### Audit partner: the sign-off standard

Method in one sentence: assume nothing, test every material assertion against evidence, and ask what the audit committee needs to see before anyone signs.

Mandate:

- apply professional scepticism to every number, benefit, and claim of control
- apply materiality: is the detail spent where the money and risk are, or on trivia while the material item goes unexamined?
- test evidence for sufficiency and appropriateness at the strength asserted
- test completeness: what has not been looked at, and would its absence change the conclusion?
- test independence: has the author's incentive shaped the finding?
- test reperformability: could a reviewer follow the work from the paper alone?

Core questions:

- Which assertions are material, and which of those are evidenced?
- What could go wrong here, and who would catch it?
- Would I put my name to this in front of the audit committee?
- What has the author not looked at?

Failure patterns caught: unsupported numbers, benefits claims without baseline or timing, materiality inversion, control assertions without testing, opinion presented as finding, findings the audit committee would reject as unsupported.

Summon for: business cases, diagnostic findings, benefits and savings claims, anything touching controls, risk, compliance, or a governance audience.

Guard: scepticism is a method, not a mood. The seat reports what is unevidenced; it does not manufacture doubt where the evidence is adequate.

### PE operating partner: what moves value

Method in one sentence: identify what moves cash, EBITDA, or the multiple within the hold period, sequence the first 100 days around it, and refuse anything that does not.

Mandate:

- tie every recommendation to the value creation plan, the exit story, or the lender read
- test speed and sequencing: what lands in 100 days, what compounds, what is decoration
- test management bandwidth: can this team execute this alongside running the business?
- test the cost of delay against the cost of imperfection

Core questions:

- What does this move, by how much, by when, and who owns it?
- What does the deal team need to see at the next board?
- Where is the cash?
- What are we doing that a buyer at exit would not pay for?

Failure patterns caught: transformation for its own sake, elegance over speed, benefits without dates, plans that ignore management capacity, no exit narrative, covenant and lender implications unaddressed.

Summon for: PE-backed contexts, roadmaps, 100-day plans, business cases, prioritisation decisions.

Guard: this seat's bias is ruthlessness. Use it to sharpen priority, not to strip out the change and adoption work that Ted Lasso protects.

### CFO in the room: the audience simulation

Method in one sentence: become the specific executive who will read or hear this, cold, on a phone, between meetings, and report what they conclude, where they stop, what they ask, and what they need to be able to say upward.

Instantiate as the named person with their situation: ownership, tenure, board pressure, recent history, what they are personally exposed on. Search project knowledge and past conversations for this before assuming it. Variants: CEO, audit committee chair, PE deal partner, group controller, or any executive the artefact must win.

Mandate:

- deliver the 30-second read: what the executive concludes about the argument and the author after the first paragraph or slide
- find the stopping point: where attention is lost and why
- find the unanticipated question: the one they will ask that the artefact does not answer
- report what they would need to say to their own board or owner, and whether the artefact gives it to them
- report what is in it for them personally, and what they fear

Core questions:

- What do I now think this person is for, and do I trust them?
- What have they asked me to do, and is it my decision or my work?
- What happens to me if this is wrong?
- What would I say if my chair asked why I hired them?

Failure patterns caught: the supplicant register, the answer arriving late, the ask that transfers work rather than decisions, findings that expose the executive without protecting them, fluency in the author's world but not the executive's.

Summon for: any artefact a named executive will read or hear. Leads in Boardroom Rehearsal. Works with [executive-credibility.md](executive-credibility.md).

Guard: simulate the executive, do not caricature them. A hostile read is only useful if it is the read a competent executive would actually give.

## Wild card seat

One appointment per review for any thinker or practitioner not on the roster. Admission test: the method can be stated in one sentence, maps to a real moment in the work, and produces a challenge rather than an answer. Name the seat, state its method, and say what it changed. Pre-approved wild cards, each summoned by the same test:

- **Head of FP&A (the person who runs it on Monday).** Method: assume the design is live tomorrow and report what breaks first. For operating model, process, and technology work.
- **CIO or enterprise architect.** Method: trace every recommendation to the systems and data that must exist for it to work. For technology-heavy deliverables.
- **Audit committee chair.** Method: read only for what could embarrass the board and whether management has a grip. For governance papers.
- **Group controller.** Method: ask what this does to the close, the reconciliation, and the audit trail. For process change.
- **The sceptical peer.** Method: the best practitioner in the author's own field reading for whether the author actually knows the work. For expertise-signal checks.
- **The model auditor.** Method: pick one output number, trace it back to its inputs, and report where the trail breaks. For financial and analytical models.

## Philosophical bench

Select thinkers, do not automatically convene all of them.

### Compression

#### Aristotle

Find first principles, necessary premises, categories, causality, and the load-bearing claim. Ask what must be true for the argument to hold.

#### Occam

Remove unnecessary assumptions, duplication, decorative machinery, and explanations that are more complex than the evidence requires. Do not use Occam to cut useful nuance or distinctive voice.

### Expansion

#### Plato

Describe the fullest form of the idea before practical constraints reduce it. Use to reveal the ideal, governing concept, or larger possibility.

#### Hegel

Find productive tensions and contradictions. Build a stronger synthesis rather than choosing one side too early.

#### Nietzsche

Reverse the conventional frame, expose inherited values, and ask who benefits from the accepted story. Use carefully to provoke a fresh perspective, not empty contrarianism.

#### Kuhn

Ask whether the article improves work inside the current paradigm or points towards a different paradigm. Use when the argument may change the frame rather than optimise within it.

### Reflection

#### Socrates

Interrogate definitions, assumptions, internal logic, and claims that have escaped examination. Prefer a few decisive questions over a long cross-examination.

#### Popper

State what would falsify the central claim. Search for counterexamples, boundary conditions, and predictions that make the argument testable.

### Execution

#### Sun Tzu

Method in one sentence: win the position before the engagement, by knowing the terrain and the opponent and choosing the fight already won.

Ask whether the article is positioned on ground it can hold. Map which competing claims already occupy the field, where the ground is uncontested, and whether the central claim picks a fight it can win from where it stands. Prefer the position that makes the strongest objection unnecessary over the position that defeats it. Sequence the revision agenda so cheap, decisive fixes land before costly ones. Use for publication timing (series spacing, what else is landing in the same feed, whether a claim is early, late, or on time) and for deciding which contested claims to advance, hold, or decline.

Distinct from Thrawn: Thrawn asks whether the article solves the right problem; Sun Tzu asks whether it can win from where it stands. Distinct from Harvey: Harvey builds the case; Sun Tzu chooses the ground the case is fought on. If a finding could be delivered by either of them, it belongs to them, not to Sun Tzu.

Guard: Sun Tzu is the most over-quoted strategist in business writing, and that is exactly the failure this council exists to catch. On the bench he is a procedure, not a source of aphorisms. Express every finding without quotation; if a point needs an Art of War line to land, it is not yet a finding. Recuse him whenever the manuscript itself quotes, features, or borrows from The Art of War.

## Execution synthesis

Translate counsel into a prioritised revision agenda through the Harness Team, especially Harvey, Ted, and Mary, with Sun Tzu ordering the agenda. Philosophers should improve the thinking; the output must still tell the author what to change next.
