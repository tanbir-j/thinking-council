# Close Read

Paragraph-level review of what has actually been written. The Council judges the document; Close Read judges one paragraph or block at a time. It rests on one finding from practice: the largest gains in clarity come from fixing the thought before polishing the sentence.

Close Read is an editorial quality method. It makes no claim about who or what wrote a text, and it never optimises for, predicts, or promises the result of an AI-detection tool.

## 1. How a session runs

The default is diagnosis. Text is rewritten only when the author asks.

1. **Take the block and its context.** Close Read needs four things: the block, what its section is for, who reads it, and the venue. If the author pastes a single paragraph with nothing else, infer the other three from the text and state the inference in one line. Ask once, and only if the diagnosis would change with the answer.
2. **Run three passes in order:** placement, reasoning, voice. A block that fails placement is reported as cut, merge, or reorder and goes no further. Never polish a paragraph that should not be there.
3. **Report the diagnostic** in the shape in section 8.
4. **Targeted rewrite**, only on request (section 9).
5. **Reconcile**, when the author brings back their own version (section 10).

When several blocks from one document are reviewed in a session, keep a running ledger: each block's job in one line, the illustrative cases already used, and the shape of each paragraph. The ledger feeds the document-level guards in section 13.

## 2. Keep is the default

A block is kept when its job is distinct, its claim is supported at the strength stated, and the intended reader can supply any unstated link without effort. Say so in one line and move on.

Expect most blocks in a competent draft to be kept. A review that changes everything has failed, because it has stopped distinguishing strong passages from weak ones. Never suggest an edit to show effort, to add variety, or because a different wording is available.

## 3. The spine: defect, question, operation, test

Every finding names a defect, the question that exposed it, the operation that would fix it, and the test the fixed block must pass.

| Pass | Defect | Question that finds it | Usual operation | The block passes when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Placement | No distinct job | If this block went, what would the reader lose? | Cut, merge | Its job can be stated in one line that no neighbouring block shares |
| Placement | Right job, wrong place | Does the reader already have what this block depends on? | Reorder | Nothing in it relies on something that comes later |
| Reasoning | Claim stronger or vaguer than its support | Is this a fact, a sourced finding, an attributed opinion, a proposed method, a hypothesis, or an illustration? | Qualify, make specific, query | The certainty of the wording matches the kind of claim |
| Reasoning | Missing link | Why or how does this happen? | Repair the link, query | The intended reader could say why in one sentence |
| Reasoning | No consequence | Who cares, and what changes for them? | Make specific, expand, query | A consequence is stated at a certainty the evidence supports |
| Reasoning | Nothing to check | What would a reader look for to know this is true or working? | Expand | A test, comparison, record, or decision is named where the block calls for one |
| Reasoning | Two things treated as one | Which two ideas, acts, or measures does this wording merge? | Differentiate | Each term is used one way and each act has its own owner |
| Voice | Friction in expression | How would this author say it to this reader? | Voice pass | It meets the author's style guide and nothing was added for variety |
| Every pass | Lost or altered material | Is every source sentence, number, name, and quotation accounted for? | Reconcile | The integrity check and the list of adjustments agree |

The eleven operations are Keep, Cut, Merge, Reorder, Make specific, Repair the link, Differentiate, Qualify, Expand, Query, and Voice pass. They are choices. Most blocks need none or one.

The chain "situation, link, consequence, test" is a way to check the logic. It is never a template for the paragraph. A simple point may need only one of the four, and the order on the page follows the reader.

## 4. Kinds of missing link

A missing link is usually one of six kinds. Naming the kind tells the author what to go and find.

| Kind | What is absent | Example of the gap |
|---|---|---|
| Causal step | The step that turns the cause into the effect | "Automation shortens the close." Which step disappears, and what was it waiting for? |
| Hidden condition | A condition the claim depends on | A forecast accuracy figure that holds only because one large contract renews inside the period |
| Incentive | The reason people behave as described | Teams keep a local tracker because the system of record updates overnight and their decision is due at noon |
| Prerequisite | What must be true before the action can start | A shared service cannot take on a process whose policy differs by entity until the policy is aligned |
| Permission boundary | The line between two acts treated as one | Drafting a payment run and releasing it; recommending a price and publishing it |
| Knowledge at the time | What was known when the decision was made | A hedging decision judged by where the rate ended up |

A superficially successful result often hides the gap: the total reconciles, the demonstration works, the number arrives intact. Ask what could be wrong while the surface check still passes.

## 5. Reader-relative rule

A link is missing only if this reader cannot supply it. A finance director does not need the mechanics of an accrual explained; a general manager may. Supplying a link the reader already owns lengthens the piece and reads as talking down, which the executive credibility check treats as an anti-signal. Name the reader before calling a link missing.

## 6. Who supplies the missing thought

A gap has three possible causes, and they need different responses:

1. The author knows the link and did not write it down.
2. The author does not know the link.
3. No link exists, and the claim is weaker than written.

A reviewer cannot tell which from the page, and a language model will fill all three with something plausible. So:

- **Query is the default operation for a missing link.** Ask the author the question the reader would ask. Offer a candidate answer only when marked as proposed.
- **Mark provenance on everything the reviewer contributes.** SOURCE: already in the author's text or cited material. AUTHOR: supplied by the author in this conversation. PROPOSED: suggested by the reviewer and unverified. A proposed mechanism, consequence, number, or example is never presented as established.
- **Allow the outcome "this claim does not survive".** When no link can be found, the right operation is Qualify or Cut, and the diagnostic says so plainly.
- **Illustrative examples** are labelled as illustrations. Never invent an observation, an organisation, a figure, or a quotation and present it as real. An example that carries the weight of the argument needs the author's confirmation.
- **Author challenge is part of the method.** When the author corrects a diagnosis, the correction stands for the rest of the session and is applied to later blocks.

## 7. Reclass or adjustment

Borrowing from accounting: a reclassification moves a figure without changing the total, and an adjustment changes the number.

- A **reclass** changes expression, order, or emphasis and leaves every claim intact. The reviewer may suggest it freely.
- An **adjustment** adds, removes, narrows, or strengthens a claim. Every adjustment is listed separately for the author's approval, at any level of intervention, however small.

Label each suggested change as one or the other. If the label is in doubt, it is an adjustment.

## 8. Diagnostic output

For each block:

```
Block: [first few words]
Job: [one line: what this block must do at this point]
Verdict: Keep | Fix | Cut or merge | Query first
Findings (three at most):
  1. [Defect.] [The missing link, distinction, or consequence as a plain note.]
     Operation: [...]. Reclass or adjustment. Net words: [+/- n].
Queries for the author: [what only the author can answer]
Protect: [what must survive any change]
```

Rules for the diagnostic:

- State the missing thought as a working note to the author, in plain language. Do not write the finished sentence for the page.
- A Keep verdict takes one line: the job and the word Keep.
- Three findings at most. If a block has more than three defects, the first finding is that the block is doing too many jobs.
- Net words are stated for every operation. Where the venue has a budget, an expansion names the cut that pays for it.

## 9. Targeted rewrite

Only when the author asks, and only for the block in hand.

- Rewrite the sentences that carry the defect. Leave the rest of the block word for word.
- Give one version. Mark every adjustment and every PROPOSED element inline or in a note beneath.
- Stay inside the block's word count, or state the net cost.
- Follow the author profile's working rules. Where the author writes the final prose themselves, present the rewrite as a demonstration of the repaired reasoning for the author to put in their own words.
- Close Read does not rewrite whole documents. See section 14.

## 10. Reconcile

When the author brings back their own revision, compare it with the original before judging the prose.

1. **Integrity.** Where code can run, save both versions and run `python scripts/integrity_check.py --source original.txt --edit revised.txt`. It reports numbers, quotations, names, and references that disappeared or appeared, and source sentences with no counterpart. Otherwise do the same comparison by hand. Every source sentence must be kept, changed, merged, or cut on purpose.
2. **Claims.** List what the revision now asserts that the original did not, and what it no longer asserts. These are adjustments; confirm each is intended.
3. **The repair.** Check that the defect found in the diagnostic is fixed and that the fix did not create another.
4. **Cold-reader test** (section 11).

A polished revision that lost a sentence is a failed revision.

## 11. Cold-reader test

Fidelity checks show the truth survived. They do not show the reader understood. After a targeted rewrite or a reconcile, give the new block, alone, to a reader with no context: a fresh subagent where the environment provides one. Ask for three one-line answers:

1. What is being claimed?
2. Why does it hold?
3. What follows from it?

Compare the answers with the block's job and the repaired link. A mismatch fails the edit, however good the prose. Where no independent reader is available, re-read the block cold, answer the same three questions, and say the check was not independent.

## 12. Protected material

Never altered silently: direct quotations; named people and their roles; numbers; reported case facts; named frameworks and their components; citations and the claims they support; controls and financial logic; figures and tables. Material from a contributor or co-author is diagnosed and referred to the author, never rewritten.

## 13. Document-level guards

A reviewer handed a chain will apply it everywhere. Check the session ledger for these:

- **Shape.** No run of three or more consecutive blocks with the same internal shape. If the review itself produced the run, withdraw the weakest change.
- **Cases.** Each illustrative case does its work once. A case that returns in another section must do a different job there.
- **Consequences.** Each consequence carries its own certainty. A document in which every paragraph ends in a failure reads as manufactured.
- **Useful structure.** A numbered method or a defined set of verdicts helps the reader act and is protected. Symmetry added to look tidy is a finding.

## 14. Whole documents and the meaning gate

**Triage.** Asked to close-read a whole document, start with a reverse outline: one line per paragraph stating what it does in the argument, as distinct from what it says. Then list the blocks worth a close read, usually one in five or fewer: those whose job cannot be stated, whose job duplicates another, or whose chain is broken. Work through them one at a time with the author.

**The meaning gate.** Inside Full Council and Final Gate, run the same triage silently and report up to five blocks where the reasoning fails. For each: the block, its intended job, the missing link, distinction, or consequence, the likely operation, what to protect, any claim risk, and the net word cost. Report the gate only when it finds something material. It never rewrites the manuscript.

A passage can be natural in tone and wrong in mechanism, accurate and empty of consequence, or concise and missing the step that makes it usable. The gate finds those before any style check runs.

## 15. Beyond prose

The same spine applies wherever a claim is made.

- **Slides.** The title is the claim, the body is the proof, and the so-what is the consequence. A title that names a topic makes no claim.
- **Tables and figures.** The title or caption carries the claim. Check units, labels, and whether the figure shows what the text says it shows. Text inside a figure escapes ordinary editing, so read it separately.
- **Recommendations in a report.** Each recommendation is a claim. Its link is why the action produces the benefit; its prerequisite, owner, and test are stated or queried.
- **Financial and analytical models.** Review one block of logic at a time: a driver chain, a schedule, an assumption set.
  - An assumption is a claim: check its source and its certainty.
  - The formula chain is the link: a reviewer should be able to follow one output back to its inputs.
  - Sensitivity is the consequence: which input moves the answer, and by how much.
  - Check totals and reconciliations are the test.
  - Hidden conditions are hard-coded numbers, overrides, plugs, and circular references.
  - Blurred distinctions are actual and forecast, cash and accrual, gross and net, nominal and real.
  - Report findings. Never change a number in someone's model.

## 16. Voice comes last

The author's style guide governs voice and is applied after the reasoning survives. Three cautions:

- **No synonym roulette.** Replacing a banned phrase with a synonym while the reasoning stays weak changes nothing.
- **Rhythm comes from the material.** Never add a question, a list of three, a joke, or a short verdict sentence to create variety.
- **Length follows the thought.** A paragraph may run to one sentence or seven. Where the style guide prescribes a fixed paragraph shape, treat it as a default and tell the author where the guide and the thought disagree.

Humour earns its place when it reveals the link: a dry line that exposes an incentive does explanatory work. A line that only decorates is a candidate for Cut.

## Final test

Could the author explain to an editor exactly what changed, why it is stronger, and what was preserved? If the answer is no, the revision is unfinished.
